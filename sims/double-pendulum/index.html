Double Pendulum

One rod swinging from a pivot, with a second rod swinging from the end of the first. The equations of motion are exact and written down in full in this page's source. Nothing here is random. Run the same starting numbers twice and you get the same motion twice, to the last decimal place.

And yet you cannot predict it. That combination is what chaos means, and it is the whole point of this page.

The demonstration

Press Fan of a hundred. A hundred pendulums are released across a total spread of a tenth of a degree. For the first few seconds they move as one thick line, because they are, for practical purposes, the same pendulum. Then the line frays, then it splits, then within about fifteen seconds the hundred copies are scattered over every angle and any memory of their shared start is gone.

Nothing was added. No noise, no rounding trick, no forcing. The tiny difference they began with was simply stretched, at a roughly constant exponential rate, until it was the size of the whole system. That rate is called the Lyapunov exponent, and it is the reason weather forecasts stop being useful after about ten days no matter how good the measurements get.

What "sensitive dependence" costs you

If errors grow exponentially, then buying one more decimal place of accuracy in your starting measurement buys you a fixed extra amount of forecast time, not a proportional one. Ten times better instruments do not give you ten times the warning. That is a hard limit set by the system, not by the technology.

Not everything is chaotic

Drop Release angle to 15 degrees and release the fan again. The copies stay together for minutes. At small amplitudes the double pendulum behaves like two coupled linear oscillators and is perfectly well behaved. Chaos here is a property of the energy, not of the machine.

The honest caveats

  • Integration is fourth-order Runge-Kutta with substeps. The energy drift readout shows how far total energy has moved from its starting value. While that stays small the divergence you see is physics, not arithmetic.
  • Given long enough, floating-point error alone would separate two identical copies. That is why the copies here are given a real, stated starting spread instead of being launched identically.
  • The rods are massless and the bobs are points. Real hardware has friction, and eventually stops.

Things to try

  • Set spread to its smallest value, a ten-thousandth of a degree, and time how long the fan holds together. Then set it a hundred times wider and time it again. The difference is much smaller than you would guess.
  • Watch the spread now readout climb. Its growth is close to a straight line on a log scale, which is exactly what an exponent looks like.
  • Give the lower arm a length of 1.6 and release from 180 degrees. The tip trace fills the whole disc it can reach.
