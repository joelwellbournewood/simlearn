What you're looking at

Emergence. No equation moves the dots. Each animal follows three rules — find food, avoid being food, and breed when energy is high — and the famous boom-and-bust cycles emerge from the crowd.

The cycle (Lotka–Volterra). Hares rise when grass is plentiful → more prey feeds the lynx → lynx rise → hares get eaten faster and crash → lynx starve and crash → grass recovers → hares rise again. Predator peaks lag prey peaks by about a quarter-cycle. Switch the trace to Phase orbit to see this lag draw itself as a loop.

Energy & trophic levels. Energy flows grass → hare → lynx, leaking away as metabolism at each step. That loss is why a healthy field always holds far more hares than lynx — the classic energy pyramid.

Carrying capacity. Plant regrowth sets the ceiling on how many hares the land can feed. Slow regrowth (Sparse habitat) means a low ceiling and small, fragile populations.

Paradox of enrichment. Push plant regrowth to the max and the swings get wilder, often ending in collapse — adding food can destabilize a system rather than steady it (Rosenzweig, 1971).

Grounded in real data. This mirrors the Canadian lynx and snowshoe hare, whose populations cycle on a roughly 10-year period — famously recorded in the Hudson's Bay Company fur-trade ledgers, one of ecology's longest datasets.
