What SimLearn is all about

SimLearn is a collection of simulations you can play with. Each one runs live in your browser, and every control on the screen is there to be moved while it runs.

It is built this way because people learn a system by poking at it. Reading that a flock has no leader is one thing. Turning the alignment rule down to zero, watching the flock come apart, and putting it back is another. Once you have broken something yourself and repaired it, the idea stops being a fact you were told and becomes something you worked out.

Fun is doing real work here. Curiosity keeps you with a problem far longer than duty does, and the time you spend experimenting because you want to see what happens is usually the time that teaches you the most. Every simulation is meant to reward that: turn the dials to a silly extreme, wreck the system, and see what the rules do when they are pushed.

The simulations are interactive and illustrative. They are grounded in the scientific principles that govern the real thing, and simplified enough that you can watch a principle work rather than take it on trust. Where a simplification changes what you see, the "What am I looking at?" panel inside each simulation says so, along with what to try and what to look for.

Everything runs on your own machine. There is no account, no tracking and nothing to install. New simulations are added one at a time.
